💡 The "Aha!" Insight: How vs. How Far

Local heated greenhouse production creates **over 4x the total carbon footprint** of imported field tomatoes. Transporting produce thousands of miles by truck generates far fewer emissions than using fossil fuels to heat a local greenhouse during winter.

Supply Path Production Emissions Transport Emissions Total CO₂e / kg
Local (Fossil-Heated Greenhouse) ~2.25 kg CO₂e ~0.05 kg CO₂e ~2.30 kg CO₂e
Imported (Warm Field + Truck Freight) ~0.20 kg CO₂e ~0.30 kg CO₂e ~0.50 kg CO₂e

2. Apples: Seasonal Storage vs. Ocean Transoceanic Freight

Hover over or click the supply nodes to discover how time in cold storage changes the carbon narrative for naturally abundant local crops.

Click or hover over a node on the map to view detailed emission metrics.

💡 The "Aha!" Insight: The Cold Storage Flip

Eating a local apple in Autumn yields the absolute lowest footprint (~0.25 kg CO₂e). However, by Spring or Summer, keeping local apples in Controlled Atmosphere refrigeration for 8–10 months **doubles their footprint (~0.45 kg CO₂e)**, nevertheless keeping the cold storage option competitive with moderate eficiency ocean freight. High efficiency ocean freight is only slightly cleaner or in line with our locally stored apples.

Supply Scenario Production Storage (Cold/CA) Transport Total CO₂e / kg
Local Fresh (Autumn Harvest) ~0.15 kg ~0.02 kg ~0.08 kg ~0.25 kg CO₂e
Local Stored (9 Months Refrigerated) ~0.15 kg ~0.22 kg ~0.08 kg ~0.45 kg CO₂e
Imported (Fresh Ocean Freight) ~0.15 kg ~0.05 kg ~0.22 kg ~0.42 kg CO₂e
Data Sources:
  • ResearchGate – Energy Use Assessment: Local vs. Imported Apples
  • Ecochain – Life Cycle Footprint of Fruit Imports
Replication & Methodology Notes:

Calculations reflect median values per kilogram of produce delivered to Northeast US retail markets. Functional unit: 1 kg edible produce. Model assumptions include heavy-goods road vehicle (HGV) freight intensity (~0.15 kg CO₂e/tkm), ocean container shipping intensity (~0.015 kg CO₂e/tkm), and grid-average electricity intensity for long-term industrial refrigeration.
