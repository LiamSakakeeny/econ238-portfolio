💡 The "Aha!" Insight: The Inflexion Point at $100/kWh

When battery storage costs exceed $200/kWh, the cheapest way to run a 100% renewable grid is to massively overbuild wind and solar generation and dump (curtail) up to 25% of all energy produced. Once battery costs drop past the $100–$125/kWh inflection point, grid architecture flips: storage expands from daily 4-hour peak smoothing to multi-day energy shifting, slumping generation overbuild and slashing total electricity costs (LCOE) by over 50%.

The Main Takeaways

This capacity expansion analysis reveals that grid storage is not simply a linear add-on—it fundamentally changes the optimal mix of power generation. At high capital costs, batteries function solely as short-duration peaker replacements. As costs fall below $100/kWh, battery arrays enable weekly energy balancing, allowing grid operators to eliminate redundant generation capacity while drastically reducing wasted renewable power.

Replication & Methodology Notes:

Capacity expansion optimization executed over an 8,760-hour annual load curve for the ERCOT region (420.5 TWh annual load, 85.2 GW peak). Solar CapEx baseline: $850/kW; Wind CapEx baseline: $1,250/kW; Discount rate: 6%; Lifetime: 25 years. Target renewable generation fraction: 100% with hourly reliability constraints.
